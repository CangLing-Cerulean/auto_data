\begin{abstract}
Successful data interventions need not validate the explanations that motivated them.
We implement separate updates for observed intervention value and conditional predictive evidence supporting diagnostic reuse.
A source-isolated language model proposes executable measurements from parent-model windows and conditional-response counterexamples.
Across 96 historical seed-level comparisons, aggregated into 32 candidate decisions on five tasks from two source families, we evaluate fixed-feature, ordinary-generation, and diagnostic-generation controls.
After addressing an identifiable regression extrapolation failure, four new generation calls produce eight legal window measurements.
Seventy complete policy trajectories show no total-gain prediction or selection benefit from these measurements at the registered budgets.
Conditional updating also fails to outperform strong fixed response models.
A frozen strong-prompt Agent completes 20 further decision calls and matches the fixed quadratic model at four queries, while trailing it and the exact uniform-random expectation at two queries.
The result is a reproducible negative development finding, not evidence of method superiority or independent generalization.
Forty-five fixed-prefix feedback forks show benefits from revealing the first overall response in some tasks, but no additional selection gain from revealing its local contrast.
A further 35 trajectories replace expert averaging with evidence-gated conditional interactions while preserving ordinary signal features; evidence now changes decisions, but lowers two-query gains and adds no four-query benefit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and scope}
We study intervention selection under fixed forecasting backbones and source-specific training protocols.
Our question is whether evidence about an explanation should govern its reuse independently of retaining an intervention with measured benefit.
Intervention-driven verification appears in DoVer~\cite{dover}; language-guided hypothesis and error-slice discovery appears in LADDER~\cite{ladder}.
Neither explanation verification nor conditional log scoring is itself claimed as novel mathematics.
The empirical contribution sought is additional decision value from diagnostic reuse and newly measured information.
The current experiments do not establish that contribution.

Proper probabilistic scores, including the logarithmic score, have established statistical foundations~\cite{scoring}.
Updating beliefs using cumulative losses also has a general framework~\cite{generalbayes}; our exponential weights are not claimed as new mathematics or a calibrated posterior.
CAAFE generates executable features and accompanying explanations using language models~\cite{caafe}.
LLAMBO uses language-model context and previous evaluations for sequential optimization~\cite{llambo}.
Our proposed distinction concerns conditional intervention-response evidence and parent-model measurements, and must be justified by their additional decision value.
The ordinary generator and adaptive Agent below are controlled comparators for our setting, not reproductions of CAAFE or LLAMBO.

\section{Separating recipe value from diagnostic evidence}
Let $L_P>0$ denote parent global MSE and $L_C$ child global MSE.
A target region $S$ has evaluation mass $p$ and complement $\bar S$.
All response coordinates share the parent \emph{global} denominator:
\[
 d_S=(L_{P,S}-L_{C,S})/L_P,\quad
 d_{\bar S}=(L_{P,\bar S}-L_{C,\bar S})/L_P,
\]
\[
 Y=p\,d_S+(1-p)d_{\bar S},\qquad Z=d_S-d_{\bar S}.
\]
Hence $d_S=Y+(1-p)Z$ and $d_{\bar S}=Y-pZ$.
These are two response coordinates, not three independent observations.
Positive $Z$ need not imply positive target improvement.

Before a query, freeze reference and diagnostic predictors
$p_0(Y\mid x),p_0(Z\mid Y,x)$ and $p_h(Y\mid x),p_h(Z\mid Y,x)$.
After revealing $(y,z)$, compute
\[
 E_h^{\rm cond}=\log p_h(z\mid y,x)-\log p_0(z\mid y,x).
\]
Observed $y$ is a scoring input only.
It is unavailable for unqueried acquisition.
The corresponding joint evidence satisfies
\[
 E_h^{\rm joint}=E_h^Y+E_h^{\rm cond}.
\]
When both models share the same $Y$ marginal, joint and conditional evidence are identical.
Our ablation permits different marginals.

Reliability accumulates as $\rho_h\leftarrow\rho_h+E_h$.
Reference prior mass is 1 and total expert prior mass is 1:
\[
 (w_0,w_1,\ldots,w_H)\propto
 (1,e^{\rho_1}/H,\ldots,e^{\rho_H}/H).
\]
Acquisition maximizes $w_0\widehat Y_0(x)+\sum_h w_h\widehat Y_h(x)$.
All response regressors learn from actual revealed gains.
The best measured eligible intervention is retained independently of diagnostic reliability.
Each event contributes once; a revised diagnostic has a distinct version.
These scores are predictive comparisons, not causal identification, calibrated tests, root-cause probabilities, or Bayes factors.

\paragraph{A decision boundary.}
For a true joint law $q(Y,Z\mid x)$, the expected conditional score is
\[
 \mathbb E_q[E_h^{\rm cond}\mid x]
 =\mathbb E_{q(Y\mid x)}\!\left[
 D_{\rm KL}(q_{Z\mid Y,x}\Vert p_{0,Z\mid Y,x})
 -D_{\rm KL}(q_{Z\mid Y,x}\Vert p_{h,Z\mid Y,x})\right].
\]
This identity constrains conditional $Z$ prediction, not the quality of $p_h(Y\mid x)$.
For example, suppose true mean gains for two remaining actions are $(1,0)$, reference gain predictions are $(1,0)$ and expert gain predictions are $(-1,0)$.
Let true and expert conditional $Z$ laws be $N(0,1)$, but the reference law $N(1,1)$, independently of $Y$.
Expected conditional evidence per historical event is $1/2$.
Whenever cumulative evidence is positive, the expert weight exceeds $1/2$, and mixed predicted gains $(1-2w_h,0)$ select the inferior second action.
This algebraic example does not simulate our fitted production models or identify the unique cause of their empirical failure.
It establishes that conditional prediction improvement alone gives no general guarantee for the gain mixture used here.
Separately retaining observed useful recipes protects their availability, but does not guarantee the next unobserved query is useful.

\section{Predictor and measurement interface}
The reference uses 22 common parent-state and action primitives.
Four manual experts add fixed interactions, and a strong control uses all 26 fixed features and every quadratic product.
Task-balanced ridge regression assigns each task total weight 1 and uses coefficient 1.
The revised input scale is
\[
 s_j=\sqrt{\max\{\operatorname{Var}_{\rm train}(x_j),1\}}.
\]
The unit floor prevents a near-constant source feature from effectively eliminating regularization in its original coordinate.
It is a development correction, not an innovation or guarantee of extrapolation.
Leave-task-out residuals estimate the Gaussian predictive scale, with floor 0.01 in response-ratio units.
For the large quadratic control, a weighted dual solve is algebraically equivalent to the primal ridge solve.

Parent-only measurements expose eight input statistics for every A/B window and five normalized B error statistics per seed, plus target-minus-complement error contrast.
Error vectors are averaged over the three seeds before executing a measurement.
The generator may use bounded arithmetic, smooth transformations, vector centering and normalization, and window reductions.
A and B vectors cannot be combined elementwise; reductions must precede cross-partition combinations.
No candidate response is a runtime measurement input.
Expressions have at most 95 AST nodes and cannot execute arbitrary code.

The source prompt includes all original features, source-task-out $Y$ and conditional-$Z$ errors, and parent-window summaries with quartile error profiles.
Ordinary and conditional-mismatch generation receive identical factual inputs and equal budgets.
Each source/mode combination receives one Sol-high call and may return zero to two expressions.
Target-family summaries and responses are absent.
All four outputs are sealed and published before target measurements are calculated.

The window control adds 104 fixed statistics to the original 26:
eight action-aligned group-coverage contrasts, 48 input--error correlations, and 48 products of coverage with correlation.
We test both its linear and complete quadratic expansions.
The latter has 8,645 features.
Thus a weak primitive reference is not the only comparator.

\section{Experimental design}
Hydraulic contributes three tasks with four actions and two implementations per task; ETT contributes two stations with two actions and two implementations.
Sampling and corresponding loss weighting are distinct implementations.
Three seeds produce 96 historical seed-level comparisons, aggregated into 32 candidate decisions.
The target region is the latter half of each forecast horizon, with $p=1/2$ across all windows and channels.
Original B-selected checkpoints are retained; no C-based checkpoint reselection is performed.
All A/B/C results were previously revealed, so this is retrospective development.

The measurement experiment uses family holdout only.
A generator that sees all source tasks cannot claim a within-source held task is unseen.
For each of five target tasks we run 14 complete policies:
primitive reference; original quadratic; window linear and quadratic; manual uniform and conditional mixtures; and ordinary/mismatch generation each with uniform, joint, conditional and combined-regression variants.
This yields 70 trajectories and 448 queries.
A policy registers forecasts, reveals one candidate, scores the forecast, and only then refits.
At 2/4 queries, an observed candidate must have mean $Y\geq0.01$ and NMAE gain $\geq-0.01$; otherwise retain the parent.
Each query corresponds to three logical full trainings, plus a shared three-seed parent.
Eight-query results cover only Hydraulic and are reported separately.

\subsection{Strong adaptive Agent comparator}
A separately frozen comparator makes four sequential decisions for each target task, using Sol-high with no tools.
The 20 calls see source feedback, the original 26 features, the two frozen conditional-generator measurements and their definitions, and their own previous target responses.
They additionally receive three fitted suggestions: the primitive reference, original quadratic and combined-measurement regressions.
The Agent can override all suggestions.
Inputs are sealed before each call, and the selected historical outcome is revealed only after output validation.
Final adoption remains deterministic under the same gain and NMAE thresholds.
Each task has one Agent trajectory; this is not repeated sampling of an Agent success probability.

At four queries the Agent reaches the same finite-pool oracle as the quadratic model, exceeding the generated conditional policy by 4.767842 percentage points.
At two queries it trails that quadratic model by 0.762098 percentage points and uniform random by 1.284700 percentage points.
The random comparator enumerates all 308 task/budget subsets; its oracle-based evaluation is never supplied to the Agent.
The four-query ETT menus are exhausted, so their zero regret does not demonstrate efficient search.
On Hydraulic G2, a third-query observation of about 52.7\% improvement from KEEP\_SECOND/S motivates querying its W counterpart, reaching 53.794211\%.
This is a useful realized continuation, not a causal attribution to its textual rationale or evidence from a controlled feedback fork.
Because the Agent receives generated measurements, its success does not show that all uses of those measurements are unnecessary.

\subsection{Fixed-prefix feedback interventions}
We subsequently freeze a separate numerical-policy experiment with a common first query chosen by the source-only primitive reference.
For each task, three policies (original quadratic, mismatch conditional and mismatch joint) branch before revealing this result.
The branches reveal full $Y,Z$ and NMAE feedback, reveal only $Y$ and NMAE, or hide the entire first result.
All later queries are chosen independently and receive full feedback.
Missing values are not imputed: gain regression uses visible $Y$ rows and conditional regression uses complete $(Y,Z)$ rows.
With only $Y$, joint evidence uses its marginal likelihood ratio, while conditional evidence remains unchanged.
This produces 45 trajectories, 180 queries and 90 budget nodes.
The forced prefix differs from the freely chosen first query in the preceding experiment, so two-query quadratic results are not the same baseline run.

\paragraph{One possible diagnostic-to-decision bridge.}
A shared latent mechanism can supply a bridge, but it is an additional assumption.
This is a sufficient modeling construction, not a necessary prerequisite for empirical signal learning.
For $Y=a+\mu\theta+\epsilon_Y$ and $Z=b+\nu\theta+\epsilon_Z$ with known Gaussian noise covariance $\Sigma$, let $c=\Sigma_{ZY}/\Sigma_{YY}$, $s^2=\Sigma_{ZZ}-\Sigma_{ZY}^2/\Sigma_{YY}$, and $g=\nu-c\mu$.
If $V_Y$ is the posterior variance of $\theta$ after $Y$, then observing $Z$ reduces the variance of a future mean with loading $\mu_*$ by
\[
 \frac{\mu_*^2 V_Y^2 g^2}{s^2+V_Y g^2}.
\]
This requires informative residual loading and a shared mechanism that transfers to candidate gains; changing rankings additionally requires distinct action loadings.
Correct joint Bayesian updating has nonnegative expected value of free information for an optimal decision, not guaranteed realized gain for our heuristic.
Factoring the joint likelihood as $p(Y)p(Z\mid Y)$ gives the same posterior and is not a new algorithm.
Our current independent regressors do not establish these structural assumptions.

\subsection{Preserving useful features while gating diagnostic interactions}
A read-only examination of the frozen generated-expert forecasts identifies a specific limitation of the first implementation.
In all five mismatch-generation tasks, the reference and both gain experts select the same candidate at every step.
There are 27 nontrivial states; the smallest componentwise winning margin is 0.003028 response units.
No convex reweighting can change these choices, and induction gives the same complete query paths while the fit and feedback rules remain fixed.
This is a certificate for those predictors, not a general failure theorem for diagnostic learning.

We therefore freeze one subsequent implementation with a single gain regressor and a common factorial comparison.
The shared core is the original 26 features and their quadratic products.
For each fixed or generated signal $q$, source-task-balanced RMS $r_q$ defines $u_q=\tanh(q/r_q)$; a source-identically-zero signal remains unavailable.
All gain models retain the ordinary feature $u_q$ independently of its diagnostic support.
Additional implementation-specific features are
\[
 v_{q,k}(x)=\sqrt{\operatorname{sigmoid}(\rho_{q,k})}\,u_q(x)\,\mathbf 1\{\operatorname{implementation}(x)=k\},\qquad k\in\{S,W\}.
\]
Support starts at zero, is scored with fixed-gate diagnostic predictors, and updates only the observed implementation context before the next fit.
Thus loss of diagnostic support attenuates additional interactions without deleting ordinary signal information, true gain labels, or retained recipes.
These are new conditional-prediction diagnostic versions; we do not automatically validate the original generated verbal explanations.

Seven policies form 35 trajectories, 224 queries and 91 nodes.
A00/A01 use four fixed signals, A10/A11 add the two mismatch-generated signals; odd-indexed variants update conditional support, while A00/A10 leave it fixed.
Additional controls update joint support or use ordinary-generated signals, with and without conditional updates.
All groups receive complete responses and share normalization, regression structure and budgets.
The common representation and strong-core changes preclude attributing differences from the earlier implementation solely to support gating.

\section{Development history, audit and cost}
The initial scalar-combination study used four calls and retained two mismatch-generator abstentions.
It lacked conditional-$Z$ residual input and showed extreme source extrapolation.
A frozen-snapshot decomposition identified a near-constant weight-second-moment feature contributing 347.24748 response units to one predictor.
A separately registered unit-scale revision controlled this extrapolation, but conditional updating still lacked decision value.
No old result or abstention is overwritten.

Initial parent measurement extraction omitted the double energy of internal real-FFT bins.
Original tables and the finding are preserved; corrected tables change only the high-frequency column.
An independent double-sided DFT verifies all 24,255 Hydraulic input windows.
ETT checks cover table hashes, modification scope and parent B residual hashes; a second implementation did not recompute all original ETT input statistics.

The final measurement experiment passes 48,056 independent checks:
256 generated and 6,656 fixed measurements, 2,176 model components, 1,792 candidate predictions, 640 score triples, 182 nodes and 42 summaries.
An independent observation-space calculation checks the large quadratic models without invoking the production fit.
Maximum numerical discrepancy is approximately $3.70\times10^{-13}$.
Model identity is supported by request records, and ordering by hashes, code and local timestamps; these are not external attestations.
Pre-call lock defects and their repairs are preserved.

The four new calls consume 228,136 input and 11,382 output tokens.
Summed call time is 743.100087 seconds under parallel execution; the longest call is 215.859519 seconds.
Transport falls back from WebSockets to HTTPS, with no additional generation.
Policy-internal time sums to 3.441640 seconds and measurement computation to 0.213701 seconds.
These omit portions of loading, serialization and transport.
Historical query costs are known at 152/182 nodes; 30 remain unknown.
There is no verifiable monetary bill and no complete wall-clock equal-budget superiority claim.
No new full forecasting model is trained; the historical count remains 464.
Sealed D/test partitions are not evaluated.

\section{Limitations and next evidence}
The study has five correlated tasks from only two source families, with strong development exposure.
It supplies no source-level significance or independent confirmation.
One removal-based generated feature is identically zero on ETT because its action menu contains no deletion.
A generated cancellation statistic is motivated as a reliability measurement, but our fixed model places it in a mean function with globally estimated residual scale; the corresponding heteroscedastic interpretation is not tested.
We preserve this mismatch rather than redesigning the head after observing results.

A numerical fixed-prefix feedback intervention is complete; an Agent text-feedback intervention, complete costs, and independent-source confirmation remain missing.
The strong adaptive Agent comparator has been completed but does not rescue the proposed conditional-mixture mechanism.
The tested implementations do not establish H1 signal decision benefit, H2 conditional-update benefit, or H3 decision superiority.
This does not prove that every conditional diagnostic learner must fail.
Further claims require a frozen mechanism and evidence beyond this development pool, not more favorable prompts, thresholds or candidate subsets.
